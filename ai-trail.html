AI trail: Darwinbox PM take-home

How AI was used to produce D1 (decision brief), D2 (build sheet) and D3 (prototype), what was checked, and what was rejected or hedged. ← Back to the prototype

In three lines. I worked in GitHub Copilot CLI, an agentic terminal assistant running Claude Opus 5.5. It read the case, ran web searches to check that the regulation I cited was current, drafted D1/D2 as HTML and rendered them to PDF, and built and browser-tested this prototype. I used AI for speed, for breadth on the legal landscape and for code. The things a reviewer should hold me to are the framing, the recommendation, the numbers I assumed and the product boundaries. Legal search results were treated as leads to verify, not as facts. That is why D1 §5 is written as “what I’d verify, and with whom”.

Workflow, step by step

#StepWhat AI did · what I checked or decided
1BriefI gave the agent the recruiter’s email and the case PDF, and asked it to work out D1, D2 and D3 independently. It extracted the PDF text and listed the constraints: 2-page limits, a login-free D3, assumptions stated in one line each.
2ContextIt pulled in my own interview prep notes on Darwinbox’s published AI direction: Cortex, with governed agents and decision receipts. That is why the audit story is built around per-decision receipts.
3Regulation currency checkIt ran three web searches: the EU AI Act Digital Omnibus timing, US state laws (NYC LL 144, Illinois, Colorado, California) and India’s DPDP Rules phasing.
Hedged: the reported move of the Annex III high-risk deadline to Dec 2027 is written as “reported”, and the recommendation is to build to the high-risk standard regardless.
Rejected: one result gave a wrong bill number for the Colorado AI Act, so no bill numbers or effective dates are cited for US states. They appear as items to verify with US counsel.
4FramingThe agent and I worked through the core reframe: the customer is losing candidates to a slow “yes”, not a slow “no”. From there came the three options, the recruiter-hours model (assumptions stated in D1/D2) and the gates that govern autonomy.
5D1 and D2The agent drafted print-styled HTML, rendered it to PDF, checked page count and inspected page images. It fixed a table split across a page break and used the spare space for a “what we tell the head of TA” paragraph.
6D3 prototype (v1)A single-file HTML app with no backend and synthetic data. An automated test ran all routes: Advance and booking; Review from an unclear knockout; India knockout auto-close followed by a human review request; Germany knockout routed to human confirmation; the recruiter decision requiring a reason code; a policy change blocked for Germany; a denied unlock request; criteria lint blocking proxies (age, postcode) and accepting a valid criterion; receipts export. Result: 0 JavaScript errors.
7Self-review round (v2)I reviewed v1 as a reader would and gave the agent eight gaps to close (table below). It rewrote the prototype as an overview plus seven role views, split into modules, rewrote D2, and updated D1. Both PDFs stay at 2 pages.
8Re-testThe Playwright suite grew to 10 end-to-end flows: CV path to accepted offer; IN knockout; DE knockout confirmed by a recruiter; Review to recruiter advance to booking; interviewer availability and the HM sample; weight tuning, the fair alternative, capacity fit and publish; the unlock request signed by each gate owner; rank-reject after unlock; agent quality and auditor; dark theme. It caught three bugs, which were fixed: the recruiter queue did not pre-select the waiting candidate; a CSS class name collided between system events and chat bubbles; a persona check crashed on views without one. Result: 10/10 pass, 0 JavaScript errors, on the local build and on the hosted link.

Review round: gaps found in v1 and what changed

Gap in v1What changed (prototype view · document section)
Only the questionnaire path; what if the candidate sends a CV?Candidate: a CV path. PII is redacted and the CV is scanned for prompt injection. Criteria are extracted with a quote each, the candidate confirms or fixes them, and only the gaps are asked. · D1 §1, D2 R2/R4
How are interviews scheduled? What about interviewers?Candidate: slot booking, reminders, reschedule, QR check-in. New Interviewer view: availability against the shift roster, capacity against need, an evidence-only brief, a scorecard with a “should not have been fast-tracked” flag, and the hiring manager’s weekly blind sample. · D2 R7/R8
How can we backtest when nobody has answered the new screener?Three sources: a historical backtest with per-criterion coverage (criteria history can’t fill go to Review), a ~600-associate employee panel, and shadow mode. · Auditor → Validation, D1 §3/§6, D2 §5
Look and feel; candidate flow too shortNew shell with a sidebar, overview and 5-minute tour. A 9-stage candidate stepper from apply to onboarding handoff. Dark theme.
How are auto-advance weights set and tuned?TA admin → Tuning: sliders show Advance per week against interviewer need and capacity, top-performer recall, Review hours and adverse-impact ratio. “Suggest weights from outcomes”, a fairer alternative, “fit threshold to capacity”. Publishing is blocked below an AIR of 0.8. · D2 R10, D1 §7
How do we evaluate the agent itself?New Agent quality view: 4 layers (understanding, conversation, decisions, safety), a gold set per language, a release gate that blocks a regressing prompt, and red-team results. · D2 §6
Impact metrics hard to scanThe D2 impact table is grouped into five buckets: candidate experience, recruiter and interviewer efficiency, hiring quality, fairness and accountability, agent quality and cost.
Who approves the unlock gates?New Governance view: per-country gates with named owners (Darwinbox Responsible AI, DPO, US counsel, an independent bias auditor, the DE works council). Only the owner can sign. Head of TA requests, plus a kill switch and an auto re-lock on fairness drift. · D1 §6, D2 §4

All figures in the new views (gold-set accuracy, panel results, fairness ratios) are synthetic and illustrative. The people named in the Governance view are fictional role-holders.

Calls I am accountable for (not delegated to AI)

  • Not shipping model-score auto-rejection, while still matching the competitor on speed. Autonomy is earned per country through gates.
  • Germany: no automated adverse outcome until counsel and the works council agree. Starting the works council in week 1, because it is the critical path.
  • Screening frontline roles against structured criteria (from a short screener or a CV, with gaps asked) instead of CV-to-JD ranking. This is my hypothesis for why the hiring manager’s best hire would have been missed, and the backtest is designed to test it.
  • The LLM extracts evidence, but a deterministic scorer assigns the band. That makes outcomes reproducible and explainable to an auditor.
  • Auto-advance weights are owned by TA, not learned silently. The tool suggests weights from outcomes, but a human publishes them with a change note and a fairness check.
  • All numbers marked “assume” are mine and are labelled as such. They should be replaced by the customer’s data in week 3.

Limits of this AI-assisted work

  • Legal points are hypotheses, not advice. The searches surfaced inconsistent details, which is exactly why each one has a named owner to verify it with.
  • The prototype’s backtest and fairness figures are illustrative. They show the shape of the evidence an auditor would get, not a result.
  • No customer or candidate data was used. The prototype collects nothing and sends nothing anywhere.
