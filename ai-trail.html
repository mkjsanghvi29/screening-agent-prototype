AI trail: Darwinbox PM take-home

How AI was used across D1, D2 and D3. ← Back to the prototype

In three lines. I used GitHub Copilot CLI throughout. I set the problem framing; it did the legal and compliance research, drafted D1/D2 and built the prototype. I reviewed each output and steered it through several rounds of changes.

How we worked

  1. Aligning on the problem framing. Before anything was drafted, we agreed the framing. My key callouts were:
    • The customer loses candidates to a slow “yes”, not a slow “no”.
    • Frontline applicants often have no CV, so we screen against structured criteria rather than ranking CVs against the JD.
    • No model-only rejections at launch; autonomy is earned per country.
    • This is a platform product, not a one-off build.
  2. Research, then D1 and D2. Copilot CLI researched the legal and compliance background (GDPR Art. 22, EU AI Act, works councils, US AI-in-hiring laws, India DPDP) and drafted D1 and D2. I treated the search results as leads to verify, not facts, so D1 §5 is framed as “what I’d verify, and with whom”, and I dropped details that conflicted across sources. We then went through rounds of alignment and tweaks on the options, the recruiter-hours model, the gates and the wording.
  3. Prototype, then iteration. Copilot CLI built the prototype and tested it end to end in a browser. After reviewing it, I asked for:
    • a CV path alongside the questions;
    • scheduling and the interviewer’s side;
    • validation without past screener answers;
    • weight and threshold tuning against interview capacity;
    • a way to evaluate the agent’s quality;
    • the actors who sign the unlock gates;
    • a fuller candidate journey and a cleaner look.
    D1 and D2 were updated to match.

Calls I am accountable for (not delegated to AI)

  • Not shipping model-score auto-rejection, while still matching the competitor on speed. Autonomy is earned per country through gates.
  • Germany: no automated adverse outcome until counsel and the works council agree. Starting the works council in week 1, because it is the critical path.
  • Screening frontline roles against structured criteria (from a short screener or a CV, with gaps asked) instead of CV-to-JD ranking. This is my hypothesis for why the hiring manager’s best hire would have been missed, and the backtest is designed to test it.
  • The LLM extracts evidence, but a deterministic scorer assigns the band. That makes outcomes reproducible and explainable to an auditor.
  • Auto-advance weights are owned by TA, not learned silently. The tool suggests weights from outcomes, but a human publishes them with a change note and a fairness check.
  • The impact table: which metrics matter, the baselines and targets, and grouping them into five buckets (candidate experience, recruiter and interviewer efficiency, hiring quality, fairness and accountability, agent quality and cost), each with a guardrail so speed can’t be bought with fairness or quality.
  • All numbers marked “assume” are mine and are labelled as such. They should be replaced by the customer’s data in week 3.

Limits of this AI-assisted work

  • Legal points are hypotheses, not advice. Each one has a named owner to verify it with.
  • All figures in the prototype are synthetic and illustrative, and the people named in it are fictional. They show the shape of the evidence, not a result.
  • No customer or candidate data was used. The prototype collects nothing and sends nothing anywhere.
